Four Years of Physics, Photonics and Machine Learning

Abstract

I am an M.S. student and graduate research assistant at Youngstown State University working on machine learning for physical systems. Across a quantum optics lab, a photonics collaboration, an REU in generative modeling, uncertainty-aware surrogate models and a neural encoding study, one question recurs: how much should we trust what the model says? This page reports the path so far. Its outputs include papers (one under review, and one first-authored at IEEE VIS 2026), talks and posters, and coverage in Science. The main finding is that the work keeps converging on uncertainty. The main limitation is that I want to study it at PhD depth.
Keywords: uncertainty quantification · Gaussian processes · surrogate models · neural encoding · visualization · biological photonics

1Introduction

I came to Youngstown as an international student, and my first research home was a quantum optics lab full of lasers, vapor cells and noisy signals.

That lab, Dr. Michael Crescimanno's, taught me the habit that still drives everything I do: before you believe a result, check how the measurement was made. I wrote automated checks for laser power and magnetic-field drift before trusting a model of chirp asymmetry in Zeeman EIT, and that work became my first paper .

The same habit followed me into a collaboration on peacock tail feathers that turn out to lase. I built a tool to line up two very different imaging methods before drawing any conclusion about the nanostructure, and the paper ended up in Scientific Reports and in Science's news pages."Peacock feathers can be lasers," Science, July 2025. Also covered by The Business Journal and Florida Polytechnic University. A follow-up appeared in Proc. SPIE in 2026.

From there I moved toward machine learning, first at a Williams College REU on generative models and optimal transport,Summer 2024, with mentors from Caltech, CUNY and NYU. Our conditional sampling approach improved predictive accuracy for our weather models by 12%. then on surrogate models for batteries and wind turbines , where I used Gaussian process regression for the thing I care about most: not just a prediction, but an honest error bar around it.

Now, with Dr. Zack While, I ask the same question about the brain: can a neural encoding model stand in for an expensive fMRI study of how people read charts? It matched the direction of 11 of 14 published effects . I'm also starting on the uncertainty side of an open-source finite-element vibrational solver with Dr. Andrew Cooksy at SDSU.FEMvib. Early stage: uncertainty in Kriging-interpolated potential energy surfaces.
