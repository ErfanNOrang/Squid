Five versions built for a 30-second read

Same facts in every version, all read from one content file. The difference is how fast a professor gets to "is this person a fit for my lab, and did they do the work?"

The professor test, measured on every version
  1. Seeking line and dates visible without scrolling
  2. CV one click away, without scrolling
  3. Proof of fit (a result or a paper with my role) visible without scrolling
  4. Total length: how many screens to read everything
1 · Brief

Everything that decides it, on one screen

Identity and "at a glance" facts, then three area cards with their result. Details expand in place.

Best for: a general link in any email.

1.5screens3.1on phone
2 · Tailored

Opens on the professor's own area

Your email links to #sciml, #vis or #photonics; the page leads with that work and the research question that fits it. Other areas are one line each.

Best for: targeted cold emails. Try the tabs.

1.7screens3.1on phone
3 · Classic

The academic homepage they already know

Profile sidebar, a short bio, research, news and selected papers. Nothing to learn.

Best for: conservative fields. On a phone the proof sits just below the first screen.

2.9screens5.1on phone
4 · Evidence

The record as one scannable table

One row per project: question, what I did, result, paper and role. Click a row to read it in full.

Best for: reviewers comparing many applicants.

1.9screens4.7on phone
5 · Tiers

30 seconds, 3 minutes, 10 minutes

Labeled depths so the reader chooses: the decision, then the work, then everything folded.

Best for: after a Zoom call, when they come back for detail.
